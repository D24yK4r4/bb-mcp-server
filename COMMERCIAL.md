**[EUPL-1.2](LICENSE)** or **[AGPL-3.0](LICENSE-AGPL-3.0)** at the
recipient's option (see [`README.md` → License](README.md#license)).

Both are strong copyleft with a network-use clause. For most users this
is fine and free.

A **commercial licence** is offered for organisations that cannot use
copyleft software for legal or policy reasons.

---

## When you need a commercial licence

You probably need one if any of these apply:

- Your company has an internal policy banning AGPL or copyleft network-use
  clauses (Google, Apple, and many large enterprises do).
- You want to embed bb-mcp-server inside a closed-source commercial
  product or hosted service without publishing your modifications.
- You operate bb-mcp-server (modified or not) as part of a paid managed
  service and your legal team needs proprietary licence terms instead of
  AGPL Section 13 / EUPL Article 13 obligations.
- You distribute bb-mcp-server bundled with proprietary code where the
  combined work cannot be released under copyleft.

You probably **do not** need one if you:

- Use bb-mcp-server as an individual researcher.
- Run it internally inside your organisation without making it
  available to third parties as a service.
- Contribute back any modifications you make.

If you're not sure, ask.

## What the commercial licence offers

- The same code, the same release stream, the same support level — under
  permissive proprietary terms instead of EUPL-1.2 / AGPL-3.0.
- A perpetual licence for the version(s) you receive under that
  agreement.
- Optional: priority bug fix turnaround and a private channel for
  security questions (negotiated case-by-case).

The commercial licence does **not** include:

- A warranty beyond what's in the licence agreement.
- An obligation on us to accept feature requests.
- Any rights to relicense bb-mcp-server itself.

## Why we offer this

bb-mcp-server is a security tool. We chose copyleft + network-use
clauses to prevent the public framework from being silently absorbed
into closed-source commercial products — that's the rationale documented
in [`README.md`](README.md#why-this-licence-and-not-mit--apache--gplv3).

A commercial licence is the alternative for organisations that can't
work under copyleft. It funds continued development of the public
project and keeps the framework healthy for everyone.
